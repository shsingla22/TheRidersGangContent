How to Style a Polo Shirt: From the Saddle to the Street

By The Rider's Gang | March 5, 2026 | 6 min read

Two billion polo shirts are sold every year. The garment was born on horseback in 1850s India, reinvented by René Lacoste on a tennis court in 1933, and turned into a cultural symbol by Ralph Lauren in 1972. It is, by any measure, the most successful piece of sports-to-street fashion ever created.

But the polo shirt occupies a tricky space in modern wardrobes. Worn wrong, it looks like a golf-course uniform. Worn right, it's the smartest casual shirt you own. Here's how to get it right, five ways.

Style Guide

Look 1: The Italian Summer

The shirt: A fitted cotton piqué polo in white, navy, or pale blue. Skip logos — or go for a discreet one (Lacoste's crocodile, not a billboard).

The outfit: Tailored linen trousers in a light colour, suede loafers worn sockless, and a pair of aviator sunglasses. Tuck the polo into the trousers.

The occasion: A Mediterranean lunch, a rooftop bar, a summer wedding (casual dress code).

Why it works: The polo tucked into tailored trousers creates a silhouette that's smarter than a T-shirt but cooler than a button-down. The collar frames the face, and the short sleeves show just enough arm. This is the look that made the polo shirt a symbol of effortless European style.

Look 2: The Smart Casual Hybrid

The shirt: A slim-fit polo in charcoal grey, dark green, or burgundy. Cotton piqué or merino knit — both work.

The outfit: Dark tailored chinos, a navy blazer, and leather Chelsea boots. Leave the polo untucked and the blazer unbuttoned.

The occasion: A client meeting (creative industry), a dinner date, a gallery opening.

Why it works: A polo under a blazer is the modern answer to the shirt-and-tie. It has the collar, the clean lines, and the intention of a dress shirt — but with the comfort and ease that contemporary dress codes demand. The combination was practically invented for the "smart casual" dress code that nobody really understands.

"René Lacoste put a collar on a jersey shirt because he wanted to flip it up against the sun. That collar is the reason the polo shirt works in a boardroom."

Look 3: The Streetwear Edge

The shirt: An oversized polo in a bold colour — forest green, bright red, or cream. Fred Perry and vintage Ralph Lauren work best here.

The outfit: Wide-leg cargo trousers or loose straight-leg jeans, chunky sneakers or trail shoes, a bucket hat or baseball cap.

The occasion: A day out in the city, a record shop crawl, a summer festival.

Why it works: The polo shirt's subculture history is deep. British Mods wore Fred Perry polos. Hip-hop pioneers wore oversized Ralph Lauren Polo. Skinheads adopted the polo as a uniform. When you wear an oversized polo with streetwear pieces, you're tapping into decades of cultural energy. The collar stops the outfit from looking sloppy.

Look 4: The Layered Autumn

The shirt: A long-sleeved polo or a regular polo in a rich colour — camel, rust, olive. Merino wool or heavyweight cotton.

The outfit: Corduroy trousers, a crew-neck jumper layered over the polo (with the collar out), suede desert boots, and a waxed jacket or wool overcoat.

The occasion: An autumn weekend, a countryside pub, a bookshop afternoon.

Why it works: The polo collar peeking above a crew-neck jumper adds a layer of detail that elevates the entire outfit. It's a trick borrowed from preppy Ivy League style — and it works because the collar provides structure and visual interest at the neckline, where the eye naturally falls.

Look 5: The Active Weekend

The shirt: A performance-fabric polo in a technical material — moisture-wicking cotton blend or lightweight knit. Neutral colours work best.

The outfit: Tailored shorts (above the knee), clean low-profile trainers, and a minimal watch. No belt needed.

The occasion: A brunch, a yacht club, a weekend barbecue, a cycle along the river.

Why it works: The polo shirt started as sportswear — Lacoste was a tennis player, polo was a horse sport. Wearing it with shorts and trainers returns it to its roots. The collar keeps it a step above a T-shirt, and a technical fabric means it handles heat and movement the way it was designed to.

Born on horseback in India. Reinvented on a tennis court in France. Adopted by every subculture from Mods to hip-hop. The polo shirt is the most democratic garment in fashion.
