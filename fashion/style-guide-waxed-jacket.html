How to Style a Waxed Jacket: The Belstaff Guide

By The Rider's Gang | March 5, 2026 | 6 min read

In 1924, Belstaff soaked cotton in wax to make it waterproof. The idea was simple: keep motorcycle riders dry. A century later, the waxed jacket is one of the most reliable outerwear pieces in any wardrobe. It ages like leather, shrugs off rain like rubber, and looks better with every year of use.

The key to wearing a waxed jacket well is understanding what it is: a piece of riding equipment. It has a collar that stands up against wind. It has a belt to stop it flapping at speed. It has pockets that button or snap shut so nothing falls out when you lean into a corner. Respect those details, and the styling takes care of itself.

Style Guide

Look 1: The Classic Rider

The jacket: A Belstaff Trialmaster or similar four-pocket waxed cotton jacket in olive or dark brown. Ideally one that's been worn for at least a year — the patina is the point.

The outfit: Dark indigo jeans, a chambray shirt, leather engineer boots, and a knitted scarf. Belt the jacket at the waist or leave it open — both work.

The occasion: A motorcycle ride, a road trip, a walk through a market town.

Why it works: This is the outfit the jacket was built for. The four-pocket design balances the visual weight, the high collar frames the face, and the olive wax against indigo denim is a colour pairing that never misses. The more beaten-up the jacket looks, the better.

Look 2: The Layered Weekend

The jacket: A shorter-length waxed jacket in navy or black, unbelted.

The outfit: Chinos in stone or tan, a merino wool roll-neck in charcoal, and clean leather lace-ups. Layer the waxed jacket over the roll-neck as a lightweight outer shell.

The occasion: A Saturday in the city, a walk along the river, lunch at a bistro.

Why it works: The waxed jacket as a mid-layer or light outer shell gives urban weekend outfits a rugged edge without looking like you're about to climb a mountain. The fabric's sheen and texture provide visual interest that a regular cotton jacket can't match.

"Che Guevara wore Belstaff across South America. Steve McQueen wore it at the International Six Days Trial. The waxed jacket doesn't ask where you're going — it just goes."

Look 3: Smart Country

The jacket: A mid-length waxed coat in classic olive, with the collar turned up.

The outfit: Moleskin trousers in burgundy or forest green, a check shirt, a wool tie, and suede Chelsea boots. The jacket goes over a tweed or wool sports coat.

The occasion: A country wedding, a racing day, a winter walk on the estate.

Why it works: The waxed jacket over tailoring is a thoroughly British look that bridges the gap between formal and functional. It says: "I dressed for the weather and the event." The olive wax against tweed and moleskin is a palette drawn straight from the English countryside.

Look 4: The Urban Commuter

The jacket: A slim-fit waxed jacket in black, cropped at the hip, with minimal pockets.

The outfit: Black slim trousers, a grey crewneck sweatshirt, white leather sneakers, and a canvas tote bag. Zip the jacket fully and flip the collar.

The occasion: A daily commute, a coffee run, an evening walk.

Why it works: A slim black waxed jacket looks more refined than a puffer and more interesting than a peacoat. The waxed cotton sheds light rain without needing a hood, and the fabric's natural sheen gives monochrome outfits a subtle texture upgrade.

Look 5: The Adventure Layer

The jacket: An oversized or relaxed-fit waxed jacket in sand or khaki, with a corduroy collar.

The outfit: Cargo trousers or wide-leg fatigue pants, a heavyweight cotton T-shirt, trail boots or hiking shoes, and a canvas belt.

The occasion: A camping trip, a coastal hike, an outdoor market in autumn.

Why it works: The waxed jacket's roots are in adventure — motorcycle trials, overland expeditions, wartime dispatch riding. An oversized silhouette with utilitarian trousers leans into that heritage. The corduroy collar adds warmth and a visual contrast that lifts the entire outfit.

A century of riders, explorers, and revolutionaries have worn the waxed jacket. It doesn't follow trends — it outlasts them.
