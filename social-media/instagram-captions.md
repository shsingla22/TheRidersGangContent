## Post 2: Belstaff Trialmaster

**Image suggestion:** Vintage-style shot of a waxed cotton jacket draped over a motorcycle seat. Moody, dark tones. Rain on leather.

**Caption:**
Che Guevara wore one across South America. Steve McQueen cancelled a date to get his re-waxed. Belstaff bought McQueen's original back at auction for $32,000.

The Belstaff Trialmaster — designed in 1948 for the Scottish Six Days Trial, the most gruelling motorcycle endurance race in the world. Waxed Egyptian cotton. Four pockets for maps and tools. Built to survive six days of mud, mountains, and rain.

A new Belstaff is handsome. A worn Belstaff — oiled, scratched, softened by years of riding — is magnificent.

100 years. Still riding. Full story at the link in bio.

## Post 3: Gucci Horsebit Loafer

**Image suggestion:** Elegant flat lay — a Gucci horsebit loafer alongside a vintage snaffle bit, on a rich leather surface. Warm, editorial lighting.

**Caption:**
In 1953, Aldo Gucci took a snaffle bit — the piece of metal that sits in a horse's mouth to guide it — and miniaturised it in gold onto a black calfskin loafer.

The result is the only shoe in the permanent collection of the Metropolitan Museum of Art.

JFK wore them. Audrey Hepburn danced in them. Wall Street called them "deal sleds." And it all started because a man loved horses.

From riding ring to the Met. From the saddle to the catwalk.

## Post 4: Riding Boots History

**Image suggestion:** Side-by-side comparison — historical riding boot illustration next to a modern tall riding boot. Clean, editorial composition.

**Caption:**
1817: The Duke of Wellington asked his shoemaker to create a simpler riding boot. Calfskin. Close-fitted. Low heel. No decoration.

Young men across England were wearing it within a year.

2026: 55 of 57 Hermès runway looks featured riding boots. ASOS reported a 260% rise in riding boot searches. The Year of the Horse has arrived.

The riding boot was never designed to be fashionable. Every detail — the shaft, the sole, the heel — exists because of the horse. And that pure functionality is exactly what makes it timeless.

500 years. From cavalry to catwalk. Full story at the link in bio.

## Post 5: Leh-Ladakh Motorcycle Ride

**Image suggestion:** A lone Royal Enfield on a mountain road, prayer flags in the background, vast Himalayan landscape. Blue sky, barren mountains.

**Caption:**
5,359 metres above sea level. Higher than Everest base camp. Half the oxygen of sea level. Temperature swings of 30 degrees in an hour.

This is Khardung La, Ladakh. The highest motorable pass in the world.

Every year, tens of thousands of riders make this pilgrimage on Royal Enfields, Jawas, and whatever else will carry them north. The road breaks. The weather turns. The engine struggles in thin air. The body protests.

And then you reach the top. Prayer flags snapping in the wind. The Karakoram Range stretching to infinity. Pangong Tso — the world's highest saltwater lake — shimmering 134 kilometres of impossible blue below.

You don't ride to Ladakh because it's easy. You ride because the mountains don't care who you are.
