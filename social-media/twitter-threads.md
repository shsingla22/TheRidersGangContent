## Thread 1: Hermès Equestrian Heritage

**Tweet 1 (Hook):**
In 1837, a German craftsman opened a harness workshop in Paris. He made bridles for horses.

189 years later, his company is worth $200 billion and the waiting list for one of his bags is longer than most careers.

The Hermès story. A thread.

**Tweet 2:**
Thierry Hermès made horse harnesses so well that he won first prize at the 1855 Paris Exposition. His clients included Tsar Nicholas II and Empress Eugénie.

The horse was the original luxury customer.

**Tweet 3:**
When automobiles killed the carriage trade, Hermès didn't die. They took the leather skills they'd perfected on saddles and bridles — and started making handbags.

The saddle stitch they use on a Birkin bag? Same technique they used on horse harnesses in the 1800s.

**Tweet 4:**
The Birkin bag was sketched on an airplane sick bag in 1984. Jean-Louis Dumas sat next to Jane Birkin. Her straw bag spilled everywhere.

He asked what she wanted. She described it. He drew it.

One bag. 48 hours of hand-crafting. $10,000-$400,000+.

**Tweet 5:**
The Hermès logo is still a horse and carriage. They still make saddles. They still sponsor show jumping at the Grand Palais.

189 years later — the horse is still the first customer.

## Thread 2: The Gucci Horsebit Loafer

**Tweet 1 (Hook):**
The snaffle bit is a piece of metal that goes inside a horse's mouth. It helps a rider communicate with the animal.

In 1953, Gucci put one on a loafer.

It became the only shoe in the permanent collection of the Metropolitan Museum of Art.

**Tweet 2:**
Aldo Gucci noticed Americans wearing Bass Weejuns — simple, comfortable loafers. He thought: "I can do that. But Italian. And with horses."

He miniaturised a snaffle bit in gold. Placed it across black calfskin. The horsebit loafer was born.

**Tweet 3:**
Who wore it:
— JFK
— Audrey Hepburn (Funny Face, 1957)
— Jane Birkin (Cannes, 1969)
— Jodie Foster (skateboarding, 1970s)
— George H.W. Bush (White House, 1974)
— Dustin Hoffman (Kramer vs Kramer)

Wall Street called them "deal sleds."

**Tweet 4:**
In 1985, the Met Museum put a pair in its permanent collection. The only shoe to ever receive that honour.

A piece of horse equipment — on a leather loafer — in the most prestigious art museum in the world.

From riding ring to the Met. That's a journey.

## Thread 4: Leh-Ladakh

**Tweet 1 (Hook):**
There's a road in India that climbs higher than Everest base camp.

5,359 metres. Half the oxygen of sea level. Temperature swings of 30 degrees in an hour.

Every year, tens of thousands of motorcyclists ride it.

This is Leh-Ladakh. A thread.

**Tweet 2:**
The ride begins in Leh — 3,524 metres altitude. You need two days just to acclimatise. Your body doesn't want to be here. Your lungs burn. Your head pounds.

Then you get on the motorcycle.

**Tweet 3:**
Khardung La — 5,359 metres. Higher than Everest base camp. The engine loses power. The air is thin. A sign at the top reads: "Top of the World."

Prayer flags snap in the wind. The Karakoram Range stretches to infinity.

That's when you understand why you came.

**Tweet 4:**
India's motorcycle culture started with Royal Enfield in 1955. 800 Bullets ordered by the Indian army for border patrol.

The British factory closed in 1970. India kept going. Now Royal Enfield outsells Harley-Davidson globally.

**Tweet 5:**
The Bikerni — India's first all-women motorcycle club. Founded in 2011 with a handful of riders. Now 2,500 strong across 17 chapters.

In 2013, they became the largest all-women group to ride to Khardung La.

## Thread 5: Riding Boots

**Tweet 1 (Hook):**
Every detail of a riding boot exists because of the horse:

— High shaft: protects from stirrup leather
— Smooth sole: slides into the iron
— Low angled heel: prevents foot slipping through
— Reinforced inner knee: grips the saddle

And somehow, it became high fashion.

**Tweet 2:**
1817. The Duke of Wellington — the man who beat Napoleon — asked his shoemaker for a new boot. Simpler than the Hessian. Fitted closer. Lower heel. No decorations.

Young men across England were wearing it within a year.

The Wellington boot was born.

**Tweet 3:**
De Niro Boot Co. in Italy puts a one-cent coin inside every pair of riding boots.

Why? A struggling rider once found one lodged in his boot's lining — then won his class. Now every pair carries the same luck.

200 hand operations per pair. One lucky coin.
